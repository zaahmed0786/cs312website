Floriography is the language of flowers. This language became significantly popular during the Victorian Era and is often considered to be the roots of the modern floral gift-giving traditions. The color, amount, type, and arrangement are all factors that influenced the contents of the message. While the more nuanced practices are no longer common, the bouquet of twelve red roses denoting love & adoration is the most popular modern example of the remnants of floriography. Here is a list of some of the common symbolisms of the language:

  1. Flower: The species
    • Apple blossom: Preference
    • Belladonna: Silence
    • Gladiolus: Strength
    • Jasmine: Sweetness & Grace
    • Pomegranate: Deceit
  2. Color: Many species have various color variants that can denote different meanings
    • White lilies denote purity, while orange lilies can denote hatred.
    • Purple hyacinths can denote sorrow, while yellow hyacinths denote jealousy.
    • Yellow roses can denote jealousy (or even friendship) while dark-colored roses denote mourning
  3. Amount
    • Single: A simple, straightforward message focused on budding feelings
    • Three or Five: A classic small bouquet to denote affections
    • Twelve: A full bouquet representative of devotion throughout the twelve months
  4. Arrangement
    • The tying orientation of the bouquet ribbon denotes who the message is about. Left is for the giver, while the right is for the recipient.
    • If the bouquet is given upside down, then the message is inverted as well.
    • Handing the flowers to the recipient with the right hand is a "yes" while a left-handed delivery denotes a "no".
