Floriography is the language of flowers. This language became significantly popular during the Victorian Era and is often considered to be the roots of the modern floral gift-giving traditions. The color, amount, type, and arrangement are all factors that influenced the contents of the message. While the more nuanced practices are no longer common, twelve red roses denoting love & adoration is the most popular modern example of the remnants of floriography. Here is a list of some of the common symbolisms of the language:

  1. Flower: The species
    • Apple blossom: Preference
    • Belladona: Silence
    • Gladiolus: Strength
    • Jasmine: Sweetness & Grace
    • Pomegranate: Deceit
  2. Color: Many species have various color variants that can denote different meanings
    • White lillies denote purity while while orange lillies can denote hatred.
    • Purple hyacinths can denote sorrow while yellow hyacinths denote jelousy.
    • Yellow roses can denote jelousy (or even friendship) while dark-colored roses denote mourning
  3. Amount
    • Single: A simple, straightforward message focused on budding feelings
    • Three or Five: A classic small boquet to denote affections
    • Twelve: A full boquet representative of devotion throughout the twelve months
  4. Arrangement
    • The tying orientation of the boquet ribbon denotes who the message is about. Left is for the giver while the right is to the recipent.
    • If the boquet is given upside down, then the message is inverted as well.
    • Handing the flowers to the recipient with the right hand is a "yes" while a left-handed delivery denotes a "no".
